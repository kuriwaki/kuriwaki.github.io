For the past four years, I've taught a social science statistics class,
"Applied Quantitative Research Design."
Without going into all the details, I teach causal inference (econometrics) and
prediction, including difference-in-differences, fixed effects, survey sampling
and weighting, using R and revolving around exemplary papers in political science
and economics published around 2010-2023, at the level of Angrist and Pischke's _Mastering Metrics._

This year, I thought I'd document some of my reflections on the class and notes on
what I've tried,
especially how AI and LLMs should change student learning.
I was fortunate to have the Yale's Center for Teaching and Learning assign Tina Zeng '28
as a Pedagogy Partner to my class, and I thought this blog would be a good way to
document our thoughts as a teaching team (which includes other teaching fellows who may make an appearance later).

<!--more-->

The course runs from September to December 2026. Expand below for the course content and the grading criteria:

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Course topics</summary>

- **1.1** Causality; **1.2** dplyr
- **2.1** Units; **2.2** RCT paper discussion
- **3.1** Omitted variable bias; **3.2** OVB paper discussion
- **4.1** Noncompliance and instrumental variables; **4.2** IV paper discussion
- **5.1** Visualization; **5.2** Two-way fixed effects
- **6.1** Review; **6.2** TWFE paper discussion
- **7.1** Uncertainty and regression discontinuity; **7.2** RD paper discussion
- **8.1** Prediction; **8.2** Shrinkage, **9.1** ML paper discussion;
- **9.2** Standard errors, **10.1** Survey weighting; **10.2** Survey paper discussion
- **11.1** Synthetic control; **11.2** Meta-analysis
- **12.2** AI agents, big data, and advanced coding

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Grade components</summary>

(Exact weights differ for undergraduate and graduate students.)

- Attendance and pre-class exercises: 5%
- Oral understanding (mostly a oral exam): 25%
- Problem sets: 10%
- Two in-class exams: 30%
- Final project: 30%
</details>
